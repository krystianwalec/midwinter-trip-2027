🧊 Ice climbing: self-guided trips

You don't need guides, so this section covers access, conditions, beta sources, avalanche safety, and logistics. Jan–Feb is normally mid-season at all three. A strong El Niño could mean a warmer winter, though, so watch condition reports.

Montana

Bozeman: Hyalite Canyon

  • Getting there: ~677 mi, ~10–12 hr drive on I-90 (source). Or fly SEA–BZN nonstop on Alaska/Delta, ~1h40 (Alaska, route info).
  • Climbing: 250+ ice and mixed routes, e.g. Mummy Cooler I & II (WI2/WI3+). The canyon is ~30–40 min from town (Friends of Hyalite).
  • Access: main road plowed to the Grotto Falls lot until Mar 31. Plowing is irregular, weekdays only, with no sand or salt, so expect ice and use snow tires. After Jan 1, East Fork means skiing or snowshoeing in (road plowing, winter driving). Little or no cell service.
  • Beta: House of Hyalite guidebook (Joe Josephson, 2019). MontanaIce conditions forum. Montana Alpine Guides' conditions page. Hyalite SNOTEL sites (Shower Falls, Lick Creek).
  • Avalanche: check the Gallatin National Forest Avalanche Center (406-587-6981) before going.
  • Festival: Bozeman Ice Fest is Dec 9–13, 2026, outside your window.
  • Lodging: Bozeman town hotels/rentals. Ballpark not researched unconfirmed.

Feb 11–15: fits (fly) Driving uses up most of a 5-day break.

Alberta

Canmore / Banff / Kananaskis

  • Getting there: ~609 mi, ~10.5 hr drive (source). Calgary airport is about 1 hr from Canmore.
  • Areas: Bow Valley, Kananaskis, Banff/Lake Louise, Icefields Parkway. Haffner Creek (Kootenay NP) is a popular single-pitch spot.
  • Beta: IceLINES (Brent Peters), which includes ATES ratings. Ice and Mixed app (has Josephson's Waterfall Ice routes; the book itself is out of print). Conditions reports on Avalanche Canada's Mountain Information Network.
  • Avalanche: a real factor here, because many classic routes sit under avalanche terrain. Use the Ice Climbing Atlas and the Parks Canada ice/ATES guidance. Carry a beacon, probe, and shovel. Hwy 40 in Kananaskis has a seasonal closure.
  • Park fee: climbing in Banff or Kootenay needs a Parks Canada pass fee unconfirmed.
  • Lodging: Canmore hotels/condos. Ballpark not researched unconfirmed.

Feb 11–15: fits (drive) 0 weekdays off. This is the biggest selection of terrain of the three.

Colorado

Ouray Ice Park

  • Getting there: SEA → Denver → Montrose (MTJ) on United (DEN–MTJ, Southwest SEA–MTJ page). Then ~40 mi, ~1 hr drive (Visit Ouray).
  • Climbing: a human-made ice park in the Uncompahgre Gorge with 150+ named routes, walkable from town. Free to climb, no permit. Optional membership is $50–500 (Basic $90 adds early-morning access) (FAQ).
  • Season: ice farming starts in November and the park is open 3+ months (source). Weekly reports and webcams are on the conditions page.
  • Festival: Ouray Ice Festival is Jan 28–31, 2027 (Thu–Sun). Details are due in early November. Expect crowds; weekends are busy anyway.
  • Avalanche: the farmed gorge is low commitment. Backcountry ice nearby, like the Silverton area, needs normal avalanche precautions.
  • Lodging: in-town inns, e.g. Twin Peaks Lodge & Hot Springs, Main Street Inn (10% off for members). Ballpark not researched unconfirmed.

Feb 11–15: fits (fly) The festival would cost ~2 weekdays (Jan 28–29).

Ice-trip basics: bring your own rack, screws, and avalanche gear for Canmore. Watch weekly conditions, since a warm El Niño winter can leave ice thin or wet. Hyalite in Jan 2026 was described as "a little wet" in a warm-winter report. For the most reliable ice in a warm year, Ouray's farmed ice is the safest bet.

❄️ Snow outlook (El Niño winter)

NOAA

The Oct 8, 2026 ENSO discussion issued an El Niño Advisory. El Niño is strengthening, with strong-to-very-strong odds of ~83% into winter exact framing: check link. The next update is Nov 12.

Environment Canada

The seasonal forecast favors above-normal temperatures and mostly below-normal precipitation in the BC interior early in the season (precipitation).

Japan Meteorological Agency

The JMA El Niño outlook expects a weaker winter monsoon. Hokkaido's signal is milder than Honshu's (summary), which favors Hokkaido over Honshu.
